    How it works

    The AI model never writes your citations. It only reads them.

    1. The AI reads your footnotes

      An AI model reads each footnote and pulls out its parts: names, years, report series, pinpoints. Only the footnotes are sent to it.

    2. Tested code formats them

      Ordinary code, checked against the Guide's own worked examples, rebuilds every citation and decides where Ibid, (n x) and short titles go.

    3. Nothing is guessed

      The result is checked so nothing is dropped and no number is invented. If a footnote can't be fully accounted for, it is left as you wrote it and marked Check.

    Questions

    Is this allowed under my university's academic integrity rules?

    It changes formatting only: the punctuation, order, italics and abbreviations of your citations. It never touches your argument, your quotations or your choice of sources, and it doesn't write anything for you.

    Rules about AI tools differ between universities and units. Check your unit's AI policy before you use it on assessed work, and ask your lecturer if you're unsure.

    What happens to my essay?

    Your .docx is uploaded so your corrected copy can be made. Only the footnotes are sent to the AI model to be read; the body text is checked by our own code on our server.

    There's no account, so nothing is linked to your name. The privacy page says what is kept and for how long.

    What does Check mean?

    Check marks a footnote you should look at yourself: a missing pinpoint, a detail your footnote doesn't have, an abbreviation it doesn't recognise, or a footnote the AI model couldn't read.

    Where something is missing, it is marked [MISSING: …] instead of being made up. A footnote that couldn't be read is left exactly as you wrote it.

    Can I accept or reject the changes?

    Yes. With "Show edits as tracked changes" on, every edit is a Word tracked change, so you can review them one by one in Word's Review tab, or accept them all at once.

    Why not just use Obiter?

    Obiter is a good tool for building citations as you write. Use it while drafting; use us when you're done, to check every footnote in the finished essay, add Ibid and (n x) where they belong, and fix the ones written by hand.

    Which file types work?

    Word .docx files up to 10 MB, with real Word footnotes. If you write in Pages or Google Docs, export to Word (.docx) first. An older .doc file needs to be saved as .docx in Word.
