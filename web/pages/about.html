How it works

An AI model reads each footnote and pulls out its parts: party names, years, report series, authors, pinpoints. It doesn't write the citation. Ordinary code then builds every citation to AGLC4 and decides where Ibid, (n x) and short titles go across the whole essay.

That code is tested against the Guide's own worked examples: 68 from the chapter on cases, 40 from legislative materials and 12 on subsequent references, alongside tests for secondary sources and treaties. The result is checked so nothing in a footnote is dropped and no number is invented. If a footnote can't be fully accounted for, it's left exactly as written and marked Check.

What it doesn't do

  • It doesn't change your argument, your quotations or which sources you cite.
  • It doesn't look up sources or fill in details you didn't give. A missing year is marked [MISSING: year], never made up.
  • It doesn't cover every AGLC4 rule. What it fixes lists the rules it handles.
