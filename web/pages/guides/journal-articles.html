How to cite a journal article in AGLC4

In AGLC4, a journal article is cited as: author, ‘title in single quotation marks’, (year), volume and issue, journal name in italics, first page, then the pinpoint page after a comma. For example: Stephen Gageler, ‘Foundations of Australian Federalism and the Role of Judicial Review’ (1987) 17 Federal Law Review 162, 165.

The pattern

  • Author. First name first, as the article gives it. Join two or three authors with commas and "and"; for more than three, give the first author and "et al" (r 4.1.2).
  • Title. In single quotation marks and roman (not italic) type (r 5.2).
  • Year. In round brackets when the journal numbers its volumes, as most do. When the journal has no volume numbers and the year identifies the volume, the year goes in square brackets instead (r 5.3).
  • Volume and issue. A numbered issue goes in brackets straight after the volume with no space: 16(4). An issue named rather than numbered, such as a month or season, is separated by a space (r 5.4). Leave the issue out if the journal doesn't have one.
  • Journal. Its full name in italics (r 5.5).
  • Pages. The first page of the article, then a comma and the page you rely on (r 5.7). There is no "at" and no "p".

Articles you read online

An article you read on a database such as HeinOnline, Westlaw or AustLII is cited exactly as it appears in the journal: the database and its URL aren't part of the citation. A journal published only online follows the same pattern, with any variations covered in r 5.10. An article accepted but not yet published takes "(forthcoming)", and one published online ahead of its issue takes "(advance)", in place of the first page (r 5.11).

Common questions

Do I italicise the article title in AGLC4?

No. The article's title goes in single quotation marks in roman type. Only the journal's name is italic.

Do I include a URL or DOI for a journal article?

Not for an article you read on a database: cite it as it appears in the journal, without the database name or its URL.

Do I abbreviate the journal's name?

No. AGLC4 gives the journal's full name, in italics, such as Federal Law Review (r 5.5).

Where does the pinpoint go?

After the first page of the article, separated by a comma: 162, 165. In a later reference it follows "(n x)" with no comma: Mason (n 4) 7.
