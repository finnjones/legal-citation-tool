AGLC4 pinpoint references: pages, paragraphs and sections

A pinpoint tells the reader exactly where in a source your point is found. When a footnote supports a particular proposition, AGLC4 expects the page or paragraph you rely on (r 1.1.6), and the way it is written depends on the kind of source.

Cases: pages and paragraphs

A page pinpoint follows the citation after a comma. A paragraph pinpoint goes in square brackets. If you give both, the page comes first and the paragraph follows with no comma. A judge's name goes in brackets after the pinpoint.

Ranges

Use an en dash (–) for a range, never a hyphen. In a page range, the second number keeps only the digits that differ, so 150–155 is written 150–5. If the last two digits are between 10 and 19, both stay: 215–219 is written 215–19 (r 1.10.1). Paragraph ranges are written in full, each number in its own brackets.

Legislation: sections and other provisions

A legislative pinpoint follows the jurisdiction after a space, with no comma (r 3.1.4). Use "s" for one section and "ss" for more than one, and similar abbreviations for parts, divisions, schedules and clauses. A single provision keeps the hyphen inside its own number, as in s 20-110(1)(a) (r 3.1.4), while a range of sections takes an en dash (r 3.1.5).

Books and articles

For a book, the pinpoint follows the closing bracket after a space. For a journal article, it follows the first page after a comma.

Before and after

What {{brand}} does

{{brand}} rewrites the pinpoints you have written: the comma or space, the brackets, the en dash and the shortened second number. It can't supply a pinpoint you left out. When an Ibid or (n x) reference to a case, article, book or report has none, it is marked Check so you can add the page or paragraph yourself. See also Ibid.
