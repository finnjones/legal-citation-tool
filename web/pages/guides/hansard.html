How to cite Hansard in AGLC4, with examples

In AGLC4, Hansard is cited as: the jurisdiction, Parliamentary Debates in italics, the chamber, the full date, the page, and then the speaker's name and position in brackets (r 7.5.1). For example: Commonwealth, Parliamentary Debates, House of Representatives, 13 February 2008, 167 (Kevin Rudd, Prime Minister).

Parliamentary debates

  • Jurisdiction. Commonwealth, or the State or Territory in full, such as New South Wales or Victoria.
  • Parliamentary Debates, in italics.
  • Chamber. Senate, House of Representatives, Legislative Council, Legislative Assembly or House of Assembly.
  • Full date of the debate, then the page you rely on.
  • Speaker. In brackets after the page: the name, then any position, such as a minister's portfolio. Leave the brackets out if you aren't citing a particular speaker.

Second reading speeches

A second reading speech is a speech in Parliament, so it is cited as Hansard in the form above, with the minister who gave it in the brackets. Where it helps, say in your text or footnote which Bill the speech was about.

Explanatory memoranda

An explanatory memorandum is cited by its own title, then the Bill's name and year, not in italics, then the jurisdiction in brackets and any pinpoint (r 3.7). Use the document's own title: Explanatory Memorandum, Revised Explanatory Memorandum, Explanatory Statement or Explanatory Notes.

The Bill's name is in roman type for the same reason a Bill's is in a footnote: only Acts are italicised (r 3.2). See citing legislation.

Other parliamentary materials

  • Committee reports are cited as reports, with the committee and its parliament as the author (r 7.1.2). See reports.
  • Submissions to an inquiry give the author, "Submission No" and its number, the committee or body it was made to, the inquiry's name in italics and the date (r 7.5.2).
  • Evidence given at a committee hearing (committee Hansard) starts with "Evidence to" and the committee, then its parliament, the place and date of the hearing, the page and the witness in brackets (r 7.5.3).
  • United Kingdom debates are pinpointed by volume and column rather than page (r 24.4.1).

Common questions

Do I write "Hansard" in the citation?

No. AGLC4 calls it Parliamentary Debates, in italics, after the jurisdiction.

How do I cite a second reading speech in AGLC4?

As Hansard: the jurisdiction, Parliamentary Debates, the chamber, the date and the page, with the minister's name and position in brackets.

Is the Bill's name italic in an explanatory memorandum citation?

No. A Bill's name is never italicised, in an explanatory memorandum citation or anywhere else. Only Acts are.

Do I need the speaker's position?

Give it when the speaker had one, such as Prime Minister or Attorney-General, after the name in the same brackets.
