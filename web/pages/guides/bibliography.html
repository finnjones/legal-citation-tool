AGLC4 bibliography: layout, categories and examples

An AGLC4 bibliography lists every source you cited, grouped by kind and sorted alphabetically within each group (r 1.13). Entries look like footnote citations, with a few differences.

The groups

Sources are sorted into these groups, in this order. A group with nothing in it is left out.

  • A Articles/Books/Reports
  • B Cases
  • C Legislation
  • D Treaties
  • E Other

Within each group, entries are in alphabetical order. Each source appears once, however many times you cite it.

How an entry differs from a footnote

  • No pinpoints. The page, paragraph or judge in a footnote is dropped, so only the citation of the source as a whole remains.
  • No full stop at the end of an entry.
  • The first author's name is inverted: surname first, then a comma, then the given names. Only the first-listed author is inverted. A body that is the author, such as a law reform commission, is left as it is.

So the authors James C Hathaway and Michelle Foster, written that way in a footnote, appear in the bibliography as "Hathaway, James C and Michelle Foster".

Worked example

A footnote citing Lane v Morrison with a page and a judge, such as 235 (French CJ), gives the plain entry above in the bibliography.

Do you need one?

That depends on your assignment instructions, so check them. If your unit asks for an AGLC4 bibliography, this is the layout to use.

What {{brand}} does

With "Add a bibliography" ticked, {{brand}} builds the bibliography from the first citation of every source in your footnotes. It adds it at the end of your document under a Bibliography heading, with the groups above as sub-headings, and shows it as a tracked insertion if you have tracked changes on. Untick the option if you don't want one.
