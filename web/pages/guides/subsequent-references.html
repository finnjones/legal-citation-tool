AGLC4 subsequent references and (n x)

When you cite a source again and Ibid isn't available, AGLC4 uses a short reference that points back to the footnote where the source was first cited in full: "(n 48)" (r 1.4.1).

The pattern

A subsequent reference has three parts: a short form of the source, "(n x)" giving the number of the footnote with the full citation, and then the pinpoint. There is no comma before the pinpoint.

  • For a book or article, the short form is the author's surname.
  • For a case or an Act, it is the short title, in italics.
  • For a report by a body such as a law reform commission, it is the short title the first footnote defines, in italics.

What {{brand}} does

{{brand}} finds the first full citation of each source, then writes every later reference as Ibid or (n x) with the right footnote number. If you add a footnote early in the essay, every later number moves, so run it again at the end. An Act is cited in full each time unless a short title has been defined for it. An Ibid or (n x) with no pinpoint is marked Check (r 1.1.6).
