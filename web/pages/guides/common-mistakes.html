Common AGLC4 footnote mistakes: a checklist

The most common AGLC4 footnote mistakes are using "above n", supra or op cit instead of "(n x)", writing "at" before a pinpoint, putting full stops in abbreviations such as C.L.R., using Ibid where it isn't allowed, and leaving out pinpoints. Go through this list before you submit.

The checklist

  1. No "above n", supra, op cit or loc cit. AGLC4 refers back to an earlier footnote with "(n x)" after the short form: Mabo (n 1) 60 (r 1.4.1). "Above n" was the third edition's form; supra, op cit and loc cit come from other styles and aren't used in AGLC at all.
  2. No "at" before a pinpoint. The page or paragraph follows the citation directly, after a comma for a case or an article: 175 CLR 1, 42 (r 1.1.6). Don't write "p" or "pp" either.
  3. No full stops in abbreviations. CLR, not C.L.R.; NSWLR, not N.S.W.L.R.; eg, not e.g. (r 1.6.1).
  4. Ibid only for the footnote straight before. Ibid refers to the single source in the immediately preceding footnote. Don't use it if that footnote cited more than one source, or to point back to an earlier citation in the same footnote (r 1.4.3). See Ibid.
  5. No comma or "at" after Ibid. A new pinpoint follows Ibid after a space: Ibid 45.
  6. A pinpoint whenever you rely on a particular passage. If a footnote supports a specific point, give the page or paragraph (r 1.1.6). See pinpoint references.
  7. Judges in brackets, without "per". 129 (Toohey J), not 129 per Toohey J (r 2.4.1).
  8. A case number in square brackets. Mabo v Queensland [No 2], not (No 2) (r 2.1.13).
  9. An Act's jurisdiction isn't italic, and sections are "s". Native Title Act 1993 (Cth) s 223: the title and year are italic, the jurisdiction isn't, and there is no comma before the section (rr 3.1.2 and 3.1.4).
  10. Single quotation marks around article and chapter titles, and italics for the titles of books, reports, journals and websites.
  11. Signals in roman type. See and Cf aren't italicised (r 1.2). See signals.
  12. A full stop at the end of every footnote, including one that is just "Ibid" (r 1.1.4).
  13. The footnote number after the punctuation. In the body of your essay, the footnote number goes after a full stop or comma, not before it (r 1.1.2).

Common questions

Can I use supra or op cit in AGLC4?

No. AGLC4 uses Ibid for the footnote straight before, and a short form with "(n x)" for anything earlier. Supra, op cit and loc cit belong to other citation styles.

Is "above n" still correct?

Not in AGLC4. The third edition used "above n 1"; the fourth edition replaced it with "(n 1)". See AGLC4 or AGLC5.

Does every footnote need a pinpoint?

Every footnote that supports a particular proposition should point to the page, paragraph or section that does so (r 1.1.6). A citation of a source as a whole, such as in "see generally", may not need one.

Does a footnote end with a full stop?

Yes, every footnote ends with a full stop (r 1.1.4), even a footnote that only says Ibid. Bibliography entries don't.

What {{brand}} fixes, flags and leaves to you

  • Fixes, in every footnote it can read: "above n" and other back-references (it writes each later reference itself, as Ibid or (n x), from the order of your footnotes), "at" before a pinpoint, full stops in abbreviations, "per" before a judge, a case number in round brackets, an italic jurisdiction or "section" in an Act citation, quotation marks and italics in titles, italic signals, and a missing closing full stop. In the body text it moves a footnote number after the punctuation.
  • Flags as Check: an Ibid or (n x) to a case, article, book or report that has no pinpoint, and a footnote number in the body text that it can't safely move.
  • Leaves to you: whether a first, full citation needs a pinpoint (it never flags those), which signal fits your argument, and any footnote it couldn't read, which it leaves exactly as written and marks Check.
