How to cite cases in AGLC4, with examples

Chapter 2 of AGLC4 covers cases. A citation is the case name in italics followed by where the decision can be found, and the form depends on whether the decision is reported, has a medium neutral citation, or is unreported.

Reported decisions

If a decision is reported in a law report series, give the year, then the volume where there is one, the series abbreviation and the first page. Report abbreviations are written without full stops: CLR, not C.L.R.

The year goes in round brackets when the series numbers its own volumes, and in square brackets when the year is what identifies the volume (r 2.2.1). Some square-bracket series also have a volume number within the year.

Where a decision is reported, cite the report, not the report and the medium neutral citation together (r 2.2.7). {{brand}} gives one citation per case.

Medium neutral citations

A decision with a medium neutral citation but no report is cited by its year in square brackets, the court identifier and the judgment number (r 2.3.1). Pinpoints are to paragraphs.

Unreported decisions without a medium neutral citation

Give the court, the judge or judges and the full date in brackets (r 2.3.2).

Case names

The name is italicised in full, including the "v". These examples from the Guide show the common patterns:

  • The Crown as a party (r 2.1.4): R v Reid, Honeysett v The Queen.
  • Government bodies and officers, abbreviated: A-G (Qld), DPP (Vic).
  • Re and Ex parte cases.
  • A case number after the name, in square brackets: [No 2] (r 2.1.13).

Pinpoints, judges and later references

Pinpoints and judges' names follow the citation; see pinpoint references. When you cite the case again, use Ibid or a short title with (n x).

What {{brand}} doesn't cover

{{brand}} handles reported, medium neutral and unreported decisions as above. It doesn't format the rest of chapter 2: case history such as affirmed or reversed on appeal, proceedings not yet judged, court orders, tribunals and arbitral bodies, transcripts of proceedings, or submissions. Check those against the Guide yourself.
