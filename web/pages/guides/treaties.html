How to cite a treaty in AGLC4, with examples

In AGLC4, a treaty is cited as: its title in italics, the parties if there are only two or three, "opened for signature" or "signed" and the date, the treaty series reference, the date it entered into force in brackets, and then the article (chapter 8). For example: Convention on the Rights of the Child, opened for signature 20 November 1989, 1577 UNTS 3 (entered into force 2 September 1990) art 3.

The pattern

  • Title. In italics (r 8.1).
  • Parties. For a treaty between two or three countries, name them after the title, joined by en dashes, such as Australia–United States of America. Leave them out when the title already names them, and for a multilateral treaty (r 8.2).
  • Date. A multilateral treaty that was opened for signature takes "opened for signature" and that date; a treaty signed on a particular day takes "signed" and the date.
  • Treaty series. The volume, the series abbreviation without full stops, and the first page: 999 UNTS 171. Prefer the United Nations Treaty Series (UNTS) where the treaty is in it; otherwise use another series, such as the Australian Treaty Series (ATS) (r 8.4).
  • Entry into force. "(entered into force" and the date, in round brackets. If the treaty was signed and came into force on the same day, write "(signed and entered into force" and the date in the brackets instead (r 8.3.2). If it isn't yet in force, write "(not yet in force)" (r 8.3.3).
  • Pinpoint. After the closing bracket, with a space: art 3, art 14(1), or annex and the like (r 8.7).

Common questions

Should I cite the UNTS or the Australian Treaty Series?

AGLC4 prefers the United Nations Treaty Series (UNTS) where the treaty is published in it. A treaty that isn't in the UNTS can be cited to another series, such as the Australian Treaty Series (r 8.4).

When do I write "signed" instead of "opened for signature"?

"Opened for signature" is for a treaty that was opened for countries to sign, which is usual for multilateral treaties. "Signed" is for a treaty concluded by signature on a particular day, which is usual for treaties between two countries.

How do I pinpoint an article?

Write "art" and the number after the closing bracket, with no comma: art 14(1). For more than one article, write "arts".

Is the treaty's title italic?

Yes, and so is a short title defined for it, such as ICCPR.

What {{brand}} does

{{brand}} formats treaty citations to the pattern above, including the parties, the signature and entry-into-force dates, series abbreviations written without full stops and article pinpoints. It adds a treaty to the bibliography's Treaties group. A treaty with no series reference in your footnote is flagged Check, because {{brand}} can't look one up. United Nations documents such as General Assembly resolutions follow different rules, which it also handles.
