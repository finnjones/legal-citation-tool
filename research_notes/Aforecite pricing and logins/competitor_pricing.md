## Which AGLC-specific tools exist, what do they charge, and which are free?

### Takeaway
Every AGLC-specific tool found is **free**: the Obiter Word add-in, UNE SmartCite, the Zotero and Mendeley AGLC4 styles, and library/HeinOnline citation buttons. EndNote is paid but usually supplied by the university. Obiter is the only one that claims whole-document features close to Aforecite's (ibid, bibliography, document validation, "Scan and Repair"). Nothing found offers Aforecite's specific "upload any .docx and get tracked-change corrections back" workflow, paid or free.

### Cited Findings
- Obiter: Marketplace listings describe a "free, open-source Microsoft Word add-in that implements the full AGLC4", with 80+ source types, ibid handling, bibliography generation with AGLC4 section headings, a document check against AGLC4 rules, a "Scan and Repair" rebuild-from-document function, court mode with parallel citations, OSCOLA 5, imports (RIS, EndNote XML, BibTeX, CSL-JSON, Word Source Manager), and optional AI that needs a user-supplied API key. It collects no document content. — [Microsoft Marketplace](https://marketplace.microsoft.com/en-au/product/office/WA200010629) (developer claims; licence and repo not verified)
- Obiter's site hosts an "AGLC5 Open Letter (Original, April 2026)", so the developer is active and AGLC-focused. — [obiter.com.au](https://obiter.com.au/aglc5-letter-original.html)
- UNE runs "AGLC4 SmartCite", an AI assistant for checking AGLC4 references. UNE cautions that its advice "may not always be correct" and that the AGLC guide remains authoritative. — [UNE](https://www.une.edu.au/library/students/referencing-and-plagiarism/aglc4-smartcite); [UNE student blog](https://blog.une.edu.au/studentexperience/2025/06/24/aglc-4th-with-ai-check-your-references-and-clarify-your-understanding)
- AGLC4 "does not come standard in Endnote or Zotero. You will need to add this style manually." — [Deakin library](https://deakin.libguides.com/legal-referencing/software)
- Zotero's AGLC4 CSL has known defects: it misbehaves with medium-neutral citation data (a community fix exists on GitHub), Bill subsequent references fail, and treaties/conventions cannot be entered. — [Zotero forums](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/486661/); [Zotero forums: dual case cites](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/518346/)
- Users say Zotero "does have a good implementation of AGLC 4", but another notes the style hasn't had much maintenance. — [Zotero forums](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/479809)
- EndNote converts citations to "ibid" or "above n", but the numbers need a manual field refresh; the UTS-made AGLC4 output style is the one university guides recommend. — [UTAS guide](https://utas.libguides.com/endnote-law/insert-citation); [Curtin](https://researchtoolkit.library.curtin.edu.au/searching/legal-research/referencing/)
- EndNote 2025 student licence: $150 list, $120 sale, one-time; proof of enrolment required (currency unlabelled). — [endnote.com](https://endnote.com/buy-s/)
- Mendeley offers "Australian Guide to Legal Citation 4th edition" via style search. — [UWA guide](https://guides.library.uwa.edu.au/mendeley/styles)
- Only Library Search and HeinOnline have a specific AGLC citation format (per CDU), and "citation generators make mistakes". — [CDU guide](https://libguides.cdu.edu.au/CDUAGLC/citationgen)
- MyBib is free, with 9,000+ styles; BibGuru is free or US$4.99/mo Plus. Neither names AGLC. — [Sourcely, Jul 2026](https://www.sourcely.net/resources/mybib-review-features-limits-best-alternatives)
- Cite This For Me lists no pricing plans on TrustRadius and covers APA/MLA/Chicago/Harvard; no AGLC support found. — [TrustRadius](https://tr-web.dev.trustradius.com/products/cite-this-for-me-formerly-refme/pricing)
- AGLC5: consultation closed and no publication date announced; a guide checked 21 Sep 2026 says AGLC4 (2018) remains current. — [MLS AGLC5 page](https://law.unimelb.edu.au/mulr/aglc/aglc-5)
- Printed AGLC4 sells for A$37.25. — [CDU bookshop](https://bookshop.cdu.edu.au/products/9780646976389)

### Inferences
- Aforecite's paid competition in AGLC specifically is effectively nil. Its real competitor is **free plus effort**: Obiter, Zotero/EndNote, manual work, or ChatGPT. Pricing has to beat "time saved and risk reduced", not another vendor's price.
- Obiter's "Scan and Repair" and validation, if they work on arbitrary pasted footnotes, overlap Aforecite's core value. The owner should test Obiter on a messy essay to see whether it actually rewrites existing footnotes (Aforecite's tracked-changes, no-install workflow may still differ meaningfully).
- Reference managers only help students who entered sources into them from the start. Aforecite's niche is the late-stage "I wrote it with ad-hoc footnotes, fix it before submission" user.
- AGLC5 is pending with no date. An AGLC5 release would cause a one-off rush of demand for re-formatting, and would break the existing CSL styles, which lag (forum users expected no further AGLC4 CSL update).

### Gaps
- Obiter's licence, user numbers, and whether it rewrites existing free-text footnotes could not be verified (Marketplace page not fetchable).
- No Reddit threads (r/LawSchoolAus, r/auslaw) were returned by search, so actual student tool usage is unverified. A UTS Reddit thread "AGLC 4 reference generator" exists (mirror at twt.omada.cafe) but could not be fetched.
- LawCite and Jade were not researched in detail (both free case-citation databases, from general knowledge, not sourced here).
- Whether MyBib, Scribbr's generator, or Cite This For Me currently offer an AGLC4 style was not confirmed.
- Zotero storage prices were not collected.

## Are there human AGLC editing or proofreading services in Australia, and what do they charge?

### Takeaway
Yes, but published AGLC-specific prices are scarce. General Australian academic editors charge about **A$26–55 per 1,000 words** (≈ A$130–275 for a 5,000-word law essay). Scribbr's human proofreading works out at ≈ A$29–53 per 1,000 words, and its per-source citation editing at ~A$4/source (≈ A$200 for 50 footnotes). This is the high-end anchor: roughly 30–70× Aforecite's A$4.

### Cited Findings

Rates were not extracted. — [Monash MGA PDF](https://mga.monash.edu/pageassets/help/graduate-research/Proofreaders-for-Assignments-and-Thesis-Updated-06.2025.pdf)
- ServiceScape and editing.services advertise AGLC-specific editing; no prices were shown. — [ServiceScape](https://www.servicescape.com/editing-proofreading/aglc-editing-proofreading); [editing.services](https://editing.services/editing-proofreading/aglc-editing-proofreading)

### Inferences
- A human AGLC footnote check for a typical 3,000–5,000-word law essay with 40–80 footnotes would plausibly cost A$100–300. Aforecite's A$4 is about 1–4% of that. There is room to price higher (eg A$7–10/doc) while still looking like a bargain against human editing, if the quality is good.
- Universities restrict third-party editing of assessed work (academic integrity policies). Some students will see an automated formatting tool as safer than a human editor, which could be used in positioning.

### Gaps
- No published per-document AGLC-only price from an Australian provider was found (Capstone Editing, The Expert Editor, Proofed AU prices not retrieved).
- The SUPRA list's date and whether listed editors handle AGLC are unverified.

## Which pricing models dominate for student tools, and what are the price points and discount norms?

### Takeaway
Subscriptions dominate (monthly with a steep annual discount: Grammarly 60% off, QuillBot 58% off). Free tiers are universal, and student discounts run 10–50% (QuillBot up to 25% via UNiDAYS/Student Beans; Microsoft 50%; ChatGPT a one-month free trial at some Australian universities). Scribbr stands out for per-document, per-source, and 30-day-pass pricing. Most students do not pay for AI tools at all.

### Cited Findings
- Annual-billing discounts: Grammarly Pro US$12 vs US$30 (−60%); QuillBot US$8.33 vs US$19.95 (−58%); Trinka US$6.67 vs ~US$20. — [grammarly.com/pro](https://www.grammarly.com/pro); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/quillbot-pricing); [futurepicker](https://futurepicker.com/?p=3073)
- Student discounts: QuillBot 10/15/25% by term via UNiDAYS/Student Beans ([casrai](https://casrai.org/guides/quillbot-student-discount)); Microsoft 365 50% for verified students ([Microsoft AU](https://www.microsoft.com/en-au/microsoft-365/college-student-pricing)); ChatGPT Plus one free month at 33 Australian universities (reported) ([aivy.com.au](https://aivy.com.au/resources/chatgpt-plans-australia/)).
- Harvard undergrad survey: 30% of generative-AI users pay for premium subscriptions, typically US$15–50/month. — [arXiv 2406.00833](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.00833)
- Middlebury study: 88.0% of AI users rely on free versions; only 11.4% pay for any AI service. — [arXiv 2508.00717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.00717)
- UNI poster (n=75): perceived value was the strongest predictor of willingness to pay; academic usage frequency predicted short-term subscription intent. — [UNI ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.uni.edu/csbsresearchconf/2026/all/76)
- Trinka combines subscriptions with credit top-ups. — [trinka.ai](https://trinka.ai/pricing)

### Inferences
- Law students need AGLC fixing only around assessment deadlines (a few essays per semester), so usage is bursty. That suits per-document or short-pass pricing better than monthly subscriptions, as Scribbr's choices for its deadline-driven products (plagiarism check, AI proofreader pass) show.
- "Short-term subscription intent" tied to usage frequency supports a **semester pass** (eg A$15–25 for unlimited documents for ~4–5 months) as an upsell next to per-document pricing.
- A$4 sits in "impulse" territory: below the A$5 BibGuru-style threshold, and cheaper than a coffee and a half. Low-friction, no-account payment fits that, but per-transaction card fees (likely ~A$0.30 + %) take a large share of A$4. Packs would improve margin.

### Gaps
- No Australian-specific survey of student willingness to pay for writing tools was found.
- No data was found on conversion rates for pay-per-document vs subscription in student tools.

## Is there evidence of pay-per-document or credit-pack pricing succeeding or failing for student tools?

### Takeaway
The only solid, long-running example is Scribbr, which has kept **per-document** plagiarism checks (no subscription) and added a **30-day unlimited pass** for its AI proofreader and **per-source** human citation editing. Its persistence suggests the model works for deadline-driven student needs. No direct success or failure data (revenue, conversion) was found.

### Cited Findings
- Scribbr plagiarism check is priced per document by length (US$19.95–39.95), and "Scribbr doesn't currently offer a subscription service". — [Scribbr app](https://app.scribbr.com/plagiarism-checker); search extract of Scribbr pages
- Scribbr AI Proofreader is a flat US$9.95 for 30 days of unlimited uploads (a time-boxed pass, not a subscription). — [Scribbr help](https://help.scribbr.com/en/articles/96462-how-much-does-the-ai-proofreader-cost)
- Aggregators sometimes mislabel Scribbr's per-document price as "monthly", which shows the model is unusual enough to confuse comparison sites. — [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/scribbr-plagiarism-checker/pricing); compare [Capterra "one time"](https://www.capterra.com/p/214467/Scribbr-Plagiarism-Checker/)
- Trinka offers credit packs alongside subscriptions; Enago Reports sells access via credits. — [trinka.ai](https://trinka.ai/pricing); [Enago Reports](https://www.reports.enago.com/pricing)
- Older evidence: a 2016 BU survey concluded "students are pushing the trend towards a preference for free software". — [BU survey](https://sites.bu.edu/ithcsurvey/data/2016-2)

### Inferences
- Scribbr's mix (per-document for one-off checks, a 30-day pass for repeat use) is the closest model for Aforecite: keep A$4/doc as the entry point and add a time-boxed pass (anchor ~A$10–15 for 30 days, or ~A$20–25 per semester) rather than a deep-discount "20 uses for A$4".
- Scribbr's per-source citation editing (~A$4/source) gives an anchor for "what one fixed citation is worth". Aforecite's marketing could say "fixes every footnote for the price Scribbr charges for one".

### Gaps
- No published metrics (conversion, churn, revenue) on Scribbr's or anyone else's per-document vs subscription pricing.
- No documented failures of pay-per-document student tools were found.
